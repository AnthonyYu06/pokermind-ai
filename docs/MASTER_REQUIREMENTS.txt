1. PRODUCT GOAL AND NON-NEGOTIABLE BOUNDARIES
======================================================================

The useful workflow is:
1. I manually obtain official exports of my completed Ignition sessions.
2. I select the exported files in PokerMind and receive an import report.
3. I browse sessions, replay hands, inspect decisions, and identify study topics.
4. The system creates drills and runs simulations inside its own environment.
5. Batch data pipelines generate training datasets, train and evaluate models, and publish versioned analysis results that improve this workflow.

"Ignition-compatible" means compatible with verified exported hand-history formats. It does not mean a live connection, an official partnership, an approved third-party tool, or a guarantee of platform permission. Verify current official terms and export instructions before documenting compatibility. Reference sources are provided at the end; their contents and interfaces may change.

Mandatory product boundaries:
- Accept only user-selected static exports of completed sessions, plus clearly labeled synthetic data produced by our simulator.
- No live Ignition integration, real-time advice, HUD, overlay, browser extension, screen capture, OCR, accessibility scraping, clipboard polling, packet capture, memory inspection, or watching client logs/directories for ongoing hands.
- No automated login or hand-history download, credential collection, clicking buttons, automated betting, bot play on third-party platforms, anti-detection behavior, reverse engineering, geolocation bypass, or Terms-of-Service circumvention.
- Simulator agents may act only inside our simulator. There must be no external-game adapter or actuation interface.
- No third-party player databases, opponent deanonymization, or linking anonymous seat identifiers across sessions as persistent identities.
- No freely exposed "recommend an action for this arbitrary current state" endpoint, current-hand form, or low-latency external integration. Review requests reference a previously accepted historical decision ID; trainer requests reference a server-created simulation session and its current state.
- No production option, hidden flag, development endpoint exposed in production, or API alternate path that bypasses the offline eligibility rules.

Enforce offline eligibility on the backend, in CLI paths, and in batch jobs. Set a product minimum of 24 hours after a documented session end before real-hand analysis; apply a longer delay if current platform guidance requires it. Treat this as our conservative product restriction, not proof of platform compliance. Record source timestamps, their timezone and precision, the session-end evidence, eligibility time, and policy version. If an export cannot establish a session end, import it into quarantine and require a conservative completed-session declaration; eligibility must be no earlier than 24 hours after that declaration. Unknown, future, inconsistent, or insufficiently precise dates must not become automatically eligible. File modification time is not authoritative.

Do not pretend uploaded files or user declarations are tamper-proof. These controls prevent intended live workflows; they cannot certify what a user is doing elsewhere. Do not add invasive monitoring of the user's computer. The interface should plainly say "Offline study of completed sessions" and distinguish historical review from simulator practice.

Unknown game variants or unsupported rules must produce clear unsupported-format results rather than plausible-looking advice. Keep raw source files unchanged. Do not silently discard bad hands or invent missing information.

5. POKER ENGINE, REPLAY, AND DECISION ANALYSIS
======================================================================

Separate the full game state, public state, and each player's information state. A policy receives only its observation and legal actions. The engine owns chance events, turn order, card uniqueness, legal raises, settlement, and termination.

Use a maintained, appropriately licensed hand evaluator where practical and validate it against an independent simple reference on small/exhaustive cases. Verify rank ordering, ace-low straights, ties, board-playing ties, and impossible cards. Document deck and evaluator conventions.

Test blinds and heads-up action order, all-in calls for less, short raises and reopening rules, multiple side pots, dead contributions from folded players, uncalled returns, odd-chip allocation, and rake handling. Never compare incompatible rake or betting rules without labeling the difference. Training toy games should use their standard rules; imported games use the verified export rules.

The replayer must:
- Step forward/backward and jump by street from deterministic state snapshots.
- Show seats, hero, board, pot, stacks, bets, legal actions, and the selected action.
- Offer a decision-time view and a clearly labeled retrospective reveal view.
- Keep hidden cards hidden in decision-time view and in practice until feedback is due.
- Bookmark a decision and add it to a study queue.
- Explain unresolved parser or rule issues before showing analysis.

Provide useful descriptive metrics: hands played, net result, bb/100 with uncertainty when appropriate, VPIP, PFR, three-bet opportunities/frequency, positional summaries, street/action frequencies, and sample counts. Define denominators; opportunity-based statistics must not divide by all hands. Distinguish descriptive tendencies from proven mistakes, and avoid diagnosing leaks from tiny samples.

For analysis:
- Implement exact enumeration where tractable and seeded Monte Carlo elsewhere.
- Support legal weighted ranges, card-removal effects, multiple opponents only where supported, ties/split shares, reproducible simulation budgets, and uncertainty estimates.
- Distinguish showdown equity from the expected value of a betting decision. Equity alone does not supply a correct bet/raise/fold recommendation.
- Evaluate alternative actions using an explicit continuation policy, opponent range/model, legal action abstraction, rake model, and horizon. Keep those assumptions visible.
- Define EV consistently as expected net future chip change from the decision, or another explicitly documented convention applied to every action. Do not mix gross pot shares with net payoffs or double-count already committed chips.
- Label action-value differences as "estimated EV difference under this model" unless a validated exact reference justifies stronger wording. A sample outcome is not an optimal-action label.
- Show model version, data/snapshot version, sample support, uncertainty, and an unavailable/out-of-distribution state rather than inventing confidence.

7. SUBSTANTIVE PYTORCH AND MACHINE LEARNING PROGRAM
======================================================================

ML is a major workstream. Implement the following models with training scripts, usable checkpoints, tests, learning curves, baselines, ablations, and product integration. Do not count importing PyTorch or calling a pretrained chat model as completing this requirement.

A. SUPERVISED EQUITY MODEL

Train a neural model for expected showdown pot share given hero cards, visible board, opponent count, and an explicitly encoded range assumption. Start heads-up under a specified range family; expand only when coverage and evaluation support it. If ranges are not model inputs, state the fixed range assumption in the model contract and UI.

Generate labels using exact enumeration for small tractable subsets and a validated high-sample Monte Carlo reference for larger cases. Keep Monte Carlo label noise estimates. Use card embeddings and a permutation-aware set encoder or a well-justified MLP baseline; handle board padding, masks, suit symmetry, and impossible combinations. A deeper model must justify itself against simpler alternatives.

Compare constant/lookup baselines, a classical regressor, a compact MLP, and the chosen deeper encoder. Report MAE/RMSE, residual bias, reliability by predicted equity, per-street and rare-state errors, inference throughput, memory, model size, and speed/error tradeoffs against direct simulation. Include a held-out exact-label subset. Equity is expected pot share; if a separate win/tie probability model is used, evaluate those probabilities with appropriate calibration/Brier/log-loss metrics.

B. ACTION-SEQUENCE / BEHAVIOR MODEL

Train an autoregressive or decision-level model over the visible public action prefix and acting-player information available in the chosen task. Compare frequency tables and logistic/boosted-tree baselines with a small GRU or Transformer. Use masked legal action categories and documented bet-size buckets, with a deliberate mapping back to legal simulator actions.

The required first target is next-action distribution, useful for offline population summaries and a simulated opponent. A range-posterior head over legal card combinations or documented buckets is a subsequent extension if labels support it. Do not infer individual identities from anonymous sessions. Apply blocker masks and renormalization to any range predictions.

Report held-out negative log-likelihood, Brier score, calibration, class/support breakdowns, sequence-length sensitivity, generalization to unseen policies, and real-versus-synthetic gaps. Behavior cloning estimates what the data policy does; it is not proof of good poker strategy. Keep probability calibration separate from epistemic uncertainty and distribution-shift detection.

C. DEEP CFR / NEURAL STRATEGY

Use the Deep CFR paper as the algorithm reference [S3]. First establish Kuhn and Leduc poker with tabular CFR or external-sampling MCCFR and a trusted independent reference implementation [S4]. Then implement Deep CFR in PyTorch: information-state encodings, player advantage networks, bounded reservoir memories, external-sampling traversals, regret matching with a defined nonpositive-regret fallback, and an average-strategy network with the algorithm's sampling/iteration weights. Specify traversal probabilities, counterfactual weighting, player alternation, network reset/training schedule, and checkpoint state. Test the algorithm against the paper and small-game reference; do not substitute a DQN and call it Deep CFR.

For these tractable games, compute exact best-response metrics where available. State the exact NashConv/exploitability convention and units, including any factor-of-two difference. Plot performance against traversals, wall time, samples, and compute cost, using at least three seeds for research conclusions. Compare random/legal, simple heuristic, tabular CFR, and neural policies. Do not infer equilibrium quality merely from beating weak bots.

Only after this passes, attempt a bounded heads-up Hold'em experiment with explicit stack sizes, action/bet-size abstraction, chance handling, rules, and computational limits. Specify how observed out-of-abstraction actions are handled; unsupported states must abstain. Report approximate best-response/search results as diagnostics, not certified exploitability bounds. Never claim that a small-game convergence result transfers to multiplayer, raked, or unrestricted no-limit games.

Six-max imported hands can receive descriptive analysis, equity, and model-dependent simulation without pretending the heads-up solver is applicable. Route unsupported decisions away from strategic scoring. Provide small-game training modes so the validated neural agent is useful even before larger-game research succeeds.

D. TRAINING AND EVALUATION ENGINEERING

- Use batched loaders over partitioned shards with bounded memory; never load the entire dataset through the Spark driver.
- Log parameters, code/data versions, optimizer/scheduler, seeds, hardware, losses, validation metrics, checkpoints, elapsed time, and cost attribution to MLflow.
- Implement deterministic CPU checks, device-aware execution, checkpoint/resume, early stopping, NaN detection, gradient diagnostics, and bounded experiment configurations.
- Assess mixed precision on supported hardware without making it a requirement on macOS. Explain reproducibility limits across devices/runtime versions [S10].
- Use fixed held-out evaluation sets and document thresholds before examining final test results. Never tune on the test split.
- Run at least three seeds for final neural comparisons, show variability/confidence intervals, and report an honest negative result when the neural model loses to a baseline.
- Include ablations for sequence context, embeddings/encoding, model capacity, data scale, and leakage-sensitive feature exclusions. Prioritize informative experiments within the budget.
- Demonstrate multi-GPU/distributed training only if data scale and approved hardware justify it. Spark ETL, parallel hyperparameter jobs, and PyTorch distributed gradient training are different capabilities; name each accurately.
- A model is eligible for promotion only when correctness and calibration gates pass, its intended domain is clear, and measured quality/latency tradeoffs justify deployment. Retain the baseline as a fallback.

An LLM coach is optional and deferred. If later added, it may explain structured computed results with provenance; it must not fabricate solver values, generate ungrounded strategy labels, or receive personal histories through an external API without authorization.
